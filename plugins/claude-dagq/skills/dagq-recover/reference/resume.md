# needs_session runs: what the runtime's resume does

A `needs_session` run is resumed by the supervisor itself (`resuming (runtime)`) and taken on from there, whatever parked it: for example its landing conflicted, its verification failed after the rebase, required evidence was missing, a person answered `send_back` to an `approve_landing` ask, or a replanning parked it at a turn's end (`replan_parked`). None of it is yours: never open a resume workspace, never send anything to the session by hand, and do not `recover`, `ready` or `integrate` the run while the runtime holds it.

While a replanning guards the run's task, the runtime does not resume the run at all, nor give it up when its resumes ran out: it waits for the operation's outcome (`replans`). Once the operation ends as a continuation or a withdrawal, the runtime restores the work the park saved in a snapshot, by a path that depends on where the run works. A run in a worktree on the host has its worktree checked against the snapshot and made again if it is gone, `replan_restored` is recorded, and only then is the run resumed as above (one whose resumes ran out goes on to its recovery job or retry after the restore); when the restore fails (for example the worktree changed since, which is not touched), `replan_restore_failed` is recorded, the operation is blocked again and the run is not resumed. A run in a container has its restore handed to its executor before the resume, whether its clone is still there or not (one that is gone is made again at the base first): before the resumed turn's agent, the executor compares the clone with the snapshot and fills only a clone made again, and the runtime records its answer from the resume's slot, so `replan_restored` comes during the resume. A clone that changed since is not touched and no agent runs: `replan_restore_failed` is recorded, the operation is blocked again and the run rests out of its slot. A container run whose resumes ran out fails the restore at once (`replan_restore_failed`, blocked), whether or not its clone is there, since no turn follows in which its clone could be checked, and it is neither handed to its recovery job nor retried. A run the replanning only let rest out of its slot, with no snapshot saved, is resumed with its worktree as it is, without a restore. The runtime does each of these steps; a failed restore shows in `replans`.

After a park at a turn's end, an approval to land (`integration_approved`) or a passed review from before the park does not land the resumed run: what the resumed session adds goes through a new validation and review first.

What reaches the inbox is an ask or an attention, handled where it is described: a `decide` ask once the resumes ran out and the recovery job escalated (the supervisor applies the answer), a `stalled` ask on a resumed session (`reference/stalled.md`), and an `answer_prompt` or `send_unconfirmed` ask on an older resume, which is a past record (`reference/session.md`).
