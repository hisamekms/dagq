# The kinds of ask

Read this when an open ask's kind, options or effect is unclear (the `dagq-inbox` skill, step 3). Whatever the kind, show the ask as written and add no recommendation of your own.

An ask an AI opened (a review's `approve_landing`, plan review's `approve_plan`, goal review's `approve_goal`, a planner's `planner_question`, the observer's `blocked`) may carry that AI's `recommendation` (one of its options) and `confidence` (`high` / `low`), in `asks`, `status` and `watch` (null when it has none). Show them next to the options as the asker's, e.g. "recommended by the asker: `land` (confidence: low)", and still let the person choose: never answer with the recommendation on your own. `stats`' `recommendations` counts how often the answers chose it.

- `approve_landing`: a review's doubt: `land` / `send_back` / `cancel`. A person may add their own reason as `send_back: <reason>` (write it as given); the supervisor applies it like `send_back` and puts the reason before the review's in the run's resume. When the run's last review was a `revise` or `concern` (not a failed review), the supervisor records the answer as `review_outcome` against that review's codes for `stats` `review_reasons`; the codes come from the review, so never ask the person for one.
- `approve_plan`: plan review's concern: `ready` / `send_back: <reason>` / `cancel`. Recorded the same way as `plan_review_outcome`; never ask the person for a code.
- `approve_goal`: the goal review job's question about a goal whose tasks are all finished (it closes an achieved goal and turns gaps into drafts itself, and asks only when a person is needed: the acceptance changes, abandoning or splitting it, it cannot judge, or a fourth `gaps` in a row). On the goal's first task, no run; the question starts `Goal <ID>:` and lists the criteria not met and the gaps. Options: `achieved` (close it; refused while a task is unfinished or a follow-up from it is not settled, `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/dagq/reference/goal-close.md`), `abandoned` (close it given up; its `dependents` are never released), `gaps` (register the review's gaps as drafts on the goal) or `gaps: <what is missing>` (one gap in the person's words), `keep_open` (leave it open until its tasks, its acceptance or its follow-ups' judgements change), plus the job's own. The supervisor applies the first four (`goal_decided`) and closes the ask; an answer it cannot apply (the job's own option, other words) comes back to be carried out by hand. To have it reviewed again later: `goal review ID` on the person's word (`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/dagq-recover/reference/goal-review-by-hand.md`).
- `correct_goal`: a follow-up judged required after its source goal closed as achieved, so the goal may not have met its acceptance. On the follow-up's task, no run; the question names the goal, the judgement (acceptance items, reason, evidence) and every task the goal released with its status and runs. The runtime reopened nothing and stopped nothing. Options: `reopen` (open the goal again and move the follow-up back into it; released tasks not yet claimed wait for it again, running ones go on), `correct_verdict` (keep it closed and record that achieved was wrong; a planner puts the fix in a fix goal), `keep_achieved` (the acceptance was met; the follow-up is out of scope). The supervisor applies the three (`goal_correction_decided`, and `goal_reopened` for `reopen`) and closes the ask; other words come back to be carried out by hand. While it is open the follow-up's `judge-follow-up` and `set-goal` are refused.
  Show it in this order: the goal and that it closed achieved; the judgement as the planner wrote it (the acceptance items, the reason, the evidence); then the released tasks with their status, saying which already run or landed (`reopen` holds back only the ones not yet claimed; nothing running stops either way); then the three options. It is a person's call because it says the goal may not have met its acceptance. An ordinary membership correction (a wrong destination) never comes here, nor does a change that weakens a goal's acceptance, which comes as a planner's `planner_question` (`scope`). Add no recommendation; if the person doubts the judgement itself rather than the goal, that is their own words for the answer: it leaves the ask open and comes back to be carried out by hand. While it is open no planner can record a correction, so carry it out as `ask close <id>` first, then a planning request naming the follow-up (`request add --ref task:N`).
- `decide`: a failed, interrupted or resume-exhausted run the recovery job could not fix: `retry` / `resume` / `cancel`, no `resume` once resumes are used up, plus the job's options, which send the run back to the job.
  When the run's integrate verification failed, the runtime adds the option `edit the task's --verify, then retry_inherit` (also to the ask that opens once the job's three tries are used up). If the verify itself is broken, show it to the person. On their instruction, use `dagq show ID --full` and the failed verify log, run `dagq edit ID --verify '...'` (repeat for every command to retain, or `--no-verify`), then answer with that exact option. The recovery job runs once more, even past its three tries, sees the current verify and the `task_edited` event and can carry the commit forward. Do not answer before editing: without the edit the answer counts like any other and the ask reopens once the tries are used up.
- `stalled`: a run's headless session the runtime and its recovery job could not get on. It has no screen and takes no keys, so the question never shows a screen: it says what the session did, adds what the recovery job said when one ran (why it could not, `Why a person:`, and its diagnosis and material when it gave them), then the answers, and ends with the last turns as recorded (newest first: each turn's outcome, failure, permission denials and refused tools). It comes from:
  - a turn that ended with neither a receipt nor an open question after the runtime's nudges, once the recovery job could not fix it (`turn_without_receipt`): the question says how long ago and when it was nudged, and `wait` asks again after its next turn;
  - a turn whose tools were refused three times or more, which goes to its recovery job at once with no nudge (`permission_denied`): the same question, saying its turns were refused permissions too often to get on; the refused tools are in the last turns;
  - a recovery job's escalation of an `idle_process` alert: the question lists the processes that used almost no CPU time past the threshold. An ask a job escalated for `long_background` (background work running) has the same shape; the runtime no longer raises that alert.

  Options: `wait` (the supervisor closes it and counts again), `stop` (the supervisor has the session end through its exit request; the run then ends without a receipt and goes to its recovery job), any the job added, then `propose`. There is no `intervene` option: nobody can step into a headless session, and any other text is sent as the session's next turn (that is how an instruction is given). Separately from the options, an answer that starts with `intervene` (to an ask opened while it was still offered, or typed as text) is not sent as a turn: the supervisor closes the ask (`stall_resolved`, `answered_intervene`) and opens a new `stalled` ask on the same run at once, with the same reason and the same options minus `intervene` (and with `stop` added after `wait` if it lacked it), saying the earlier ask was answered `intervene`. Before answering, bring the person what the turns did (`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/dagq-recover/reference/stalled.md`, "A headless run's `stalled` ask"). It closes itself once the session moves on (`reference/status.md` of this skill).
- `worker_question`: typed into the worker's terminal, or sent as the next turn of a headless run (answered the same way). Its `topics` (in `status`' `asks` and the ask; the first is the primary) say what the worker left undecided, next to `reason_category`: show them with the question.
- `planner_question`: a runtime planner's question; its options follow what the planner was opened for. A draft (on the draft's task): `adopt` / `cancel` / `keep_draft`. `keep_draft` leaves it a draft that waits for a planning request; when the person says when to look again, give it a time instead (`revisit ID --at <RFC 3339 time>`, `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/dagq/reference/register.md`, "A draft's revisit time"): a runtime planner takes it up then. A finding (`finding_id`): `propose` / `dismiss`. A planning request (`request_id`, `ask --request N`): `plan` / `decline` (`reference/requests.md`). A revise: the planner's own options. The runtime delivers the answer to the planner it is about as its next turn, or opens a new planner with the answer when that one is gone; do not carry it out yourself.
- `answer_prompt`: a dialog the runtime and the job could not answer, or a resumed session's input box not ready (`input_not_ready`). No options: the answer is what to send, e.g. `enter`; the question ends with its screen.
- `stuck_exit`: a `/exit` neither could see through: `exit` / `wait`.
- `blocked`: the observer's, about one finding: `propose` (make it a proposal) / `dismiss`.
- `queue_hold`: login or cost, one per queue: `done` / `cancel_affected`. Claude's login ran out or it hit its usage limit, or no worker provider can be used (Claude held, and Codex missing or held). A run on a provider that cannot be used moves to the other provider by itself (`provider_switched`) and is in the ask only when it could not. Tell the person what to restore: log in again (`claude`, or `codex login` when the question names Codex) or wait for the limit's reset; once done, the answer is `done` (the supervisor releases every provider hold and continues the waiting runs and jobs). A Codex that cannot be used opens no ask of its own: `status`'s `supervisors[].provider_hold` shows it until its `retry_at`. What each provider event means: `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/dagq/reference/provider.md`.
- `approve_release`: a newer dagq release on crates.io, asked by a supervisor on a release build (`host.toml`'s `[update]` `release = "ask"`; `"auto"` installs with no ask). No task, no run; `subject` is the version. Options: `install` / `skip`. Show, in this order: the new version (with its crates.io link from the question); the version this queue's supervisor runs now (`current` in `status`' `release_update`); and what each answer does. `install`: the supervisor installs that release with `cargo install` in the background, checks it, applies the compatible migrations, swaps the binary (the old one stays as `.previous`) and hands the supervisors over without stopping the runs, then updates the installed plugin to the same release. The inbox and planner sessions load the new plugin only when reopened. A release with a breaking migration is not installed: an `approve_update` ask comes instead. `skip`: that version is not asked about again; the next release asks. A plugin-only one (the question starts `The claude-dagq plugin installed in Claude Code is <plugin>`; `subject` is the version the binary already runs): show the plugin's version and the binary's, and say `install` runs only the two plugin commands and leaves the binary alone. The supervisor applies the answer; when none does, it comes back as `read the answer of ask <id> and close it`.
- `update_failed` / `approve_update`, and carrying out an `approve_release` by hand: `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/dagq-recover/reference/update.md`.

The runtime applies `propose` (a planner of its own takes the finding) and `dismiss`. An answer the runtime does not apply comes back as `read the answer of ask <id> and close it` (the skill's step 4).
