# status, watch and run states

Read this when a field of `status`, `watch` or `show` is unclear.

## status

- `supervisors`: one entry per process that owns runs (`pid`, `alive`, `registered`, `mode`, `parallel`, `heartbeat_age_secs`, `stale`, `run_ids`).
  - `registered: true`, `alive: true`, `stale: false`: the resident supervisor is healthy. It is listed even when `run_ids` is empty, and picks a new candidate within a few seconds.
  - `stale: true` (`alive: false`, or `heartbeat_age_secs` over 30): it died or hangs. `up` prunes a dead registration and starts a fresh supervisor; its orphaned runs are handled with the `dagq-recover` skill, one run at a time. A registration whose PID is alive but silent is neither pruned nor reused, so `up` starts a second supervisor beside it: report the stale row and let the person stop that process (`down --force` if it is the only one).
  - `registered: false`: not a supervisor but a process holding a lease, normally an `integrate` landing a run.
  - Empty: nothing serves this queue; run `up`.
  - A registered entry also has `slots: {used, parallel}` and `waiting: {count, returning, limit}` (see "Runs waiting for a person" below). `used` leaves out waiting and returning runs, and can exceed `parallel` for a moment after a person moved a session on.
- `inbox_watcher` (also in `doctor`): whether this inbox has a `watch --role inbox` running (ADR-t906-1). Each such watch writes a record under the queue's directory and renews its heartbeat at every read of the queue; the judgment uses the heartbeat, not the process.
  - `watching`: the watches with a fresh heartbeat now (not older than three `--interval`s plus 10 seconds, and not past their own `--timeout`; a `--until-attention` watch has none and counts by its heartbeat alone). `0` means no watch is running: start the watch of `watch.md`. The Stop hook blocks your turn while it is `0`, once; start the watch, then end the turn.
  - `state`: `alive` while a watch is watching or one returned less than `grace_secs` (120) ago (the gap while you report and start the next one); `absent` otherwise. A watch that hangs or cannot read the queue counts as absent though its process is there.
  - `last_seen_at` (unix seconds; `null` if no watch ever ran) and `absent_secs` (only while `absent`): how long the inbox has gone unwatched. Tell the person when it was long: asks opened meanwhile reached nobody.
  - While it is `absent` and an ask has waited 5 minutes, the supervisor types one line into this inbox (`dagq: N open ask(s) wait for the inbox and no ... watch ... is running`) when your screen is idle and your input box empty; once more 10 minutes later, then it notifies the person. On that line, run `status --role inbox`, then start the watch as `watch.md` says, and show the open asks.
- `waiting`: the runs outside the slots (below), one entry each.
- `runs`: unfinished runs with their leases and `worktree_path`.
- `attention`: each with `run_id`, `task_id`, `status`, `kind` (the run event that brought it there), `last_error` and a fixed `next`:
  - `reviewing (runtime)`: `awaiting_integration` under the supervisor's lease: its headless review and what follows from the verdict (ADR-0027); nothing to do.
  - `review by hand`: `awaiting_integration` after `review_failed` (the headless review failed) with no open `approve_landing` ask; the person reviews it and has it integrated (the `dagq-recover` skill, section 6). A failed review normally opens that ask in the same step (with the failure and the `review.md` path), and then shows only as the ask.
  - `review and integrate`: `awaiting_integration` without a lease and without a failed review: a run accepted before the supervisor reviewed runs, or one whose supervisor gave it up mid-review on an error (`kind` `runtime_error`; its session may still be open, so read its screen and `/exit` it first).
  - A run whose review raised a concern shows only as its `approve_landing` ask (`answer ask <id>`, for the inbox); once answered `land`, `send_back` or `cancel`, the ask shows `applying the answer of ask <id> (runtime)` until the supervisor's next pass, which applies it whatever the slots and closes the ask, first recording `review_outcome` (`land` → `deviation_accepted`, `send_back` → `deviation_rejected`, `cancel` → `canceled`) when the last review was a `revise` or `concern`. After `land` the run shows `queued to land (runtime)` (`kind` `landing_queued`) until a slot and the integration slot are free; nothing to do.
  - `resuming (runtime)`: `needs_session`. The supervisor resumes it, or waits for a session still alive to end first. Once its resumes are used up, a run parked only by rebase conflicts after a passed review is retried on its old branch without asking (`auto_repaired`, `repair: inherit_retry`); any other run becomes `failed` with the `resume_exhausted` alert (`recovery_requested`) for the recovery job (`triaging (runtime)`). Nothing to do.
  - `triaging (runtime)`: `failed` / `interrupted` (or `resume_exhausted`), before the supervisor's headless recovery job acted (ADR-0047 decisions 39 and 40; its rounds keep the `triage_*` event names). The job picks one action: `retry` (only for a run with no commit of its own and fewer than two failed runs of the task), `retry_inherit` (once per task: a retry that carries the run's commits over), `resume` (while resumes are left) or `wait` (look again later). The runtime applies it only when the verdict is `repair` with `confidence: high` and its preconditions still hold (`triage_finished`, `auto_repaired` with `layer: recovery`, `recovery_finished`), then closes the run's workspace. Otherwise (`escalate`, low confidence, a broken precondition, or the alert's three tries used up) it opens a `decide` ask: options `retry` / `resume` / `cancel` (`retry` / `cancel` once resumes are used up) plus any the job added. The supervisor applies an answer that is one of the options (`applying the answer of ask <id> (runtime)`): `retry` readies the task (with or without commits: the person decided), `resume` makes the run `needs_session`, `cancel` cancels the task, and an option the job added hands the run back to the recovery job with the answer. Nothing to do.
  - `triage by hand`: `failed` / `interrupted` after `triage_failed` (the recovery job of the ended run could not start, exited non-zero, timed out, printed no verdict or failed to apply it); it is not tried again by itself. A person decides with the `dagq-recover` skill (section 4).
  - A live run's alert (`long_background`, `idle_process`, `stuck_exit`, `prompt_waiting`, `stalled`) whose recovery job failed (could not start, exited non-zero, timed out, printed no verdict) is no attention of its own: it opens the alert's ask as an escalation does (`stalled`, `stuck_exit` or `answer_prompt`, the same options), with `reason_category` `recovery_failed` and the job's failure in the question (ADR-t609-1). Its answer is carried out and the ask closes as for that kind; no job starts for the alert while it is open.
  - `recover by hand` (`kind` `recovery_failed`, `last_error` the job's error): a live run's failed recovery job that a runtime from before ADR-t609-1 recorded. A person reads the run's screen with the `dagq-recover` skill (section 4). It clears once the session exits, the workspace closes or the run is recovered (`prompt_waiting`: the dialog goes; `long_background`: the receipt arrives).
  - A `running` run that stopped at a dialog before its receipt is no attention of its own. The runtime answers a known dialog itself (Settings, or "Background work is running" after its `/exit` with a clean worktree and the receipt at HEAD; `auto_repaired` with `repair: dialog_answered`); any other is recorded as `prompt_waiting` and handed to the recovery job, which may answer a known dialog, stop the run's processes or wait. Only when the job escalates, is not sure, fails its preconditions or used its three tries does the supervisor open an `answer_prompt` ask (the screen's last lines in the question), which it closes when the dialog goes away (`prompt_cleared`), the receipt arrives or the session exits. A resumed session whose input box is not ready for its request opens an `answer_prompt` ask too (`input_not_ready`, "A text or a request the session did not take").
  - A submitted proposal's plan review shows only while it needs a person, on the proposal's first task: `plan review by hand` (`kind` `plan_review_failed`, `status` `submitted`, `last_error` the job's failure): the headless plan review failed and holds the proposal, which is not reviewed again by itself; the person readies it with the bypass or has a planner submit it again (the `dagq-recover` skill, section 8). `check the planner` (`kind` `planner_unresponsive`, `status` `revising`): the planner a revise went to did not submit the proposal again within `supervise --planner-timeout` (default 3600 seconds), or the revise waited that long for a runtime planner to take it; the person looks at that planner, whose workspace nothing closes. The same `check the planner` (`status` `working`, on its draft's task or on none) also says a runtime planner (for a draft, a finding or a revise) showed no input, no idle marker and no idle screen for that long; it shows until the planner's row closes. A concern shows only as its `approve_plan` ask; once answered `ready`, `send_back[: reason]` or `cancel`, the supervisor applies it (`applying the answer of ask <id> (runtime)`), recording `plan_review_outcome` (`ready` → `deviation_accepted`, `send_back` → `deviation_rejected`, `cancel` → `canceled`) for the concern. `plan_review_finished` carries `reason_codes` and `primary_code` like `review_finished` (`docs/design/supervisor-lifecycle/plan-review.md`, section "差し戻しの分類コード").
  - `goal review by hand` (`kind` `goal_review_failed`, on the goal's first task, `reason_category` `recovery_failed`, `last_error` `goal <ID>: <error>`): the headless goal review of a goal whose tasks are all finished could not start, exited non-zero, timed out or printed no valid verdict; the goal stays open and is not reviewed again by itself until its tasks change. On the person's word, `goal review ID` rearms it, or the person closes the goal (`goal close`) or has a planner add tasks (the `dagq-recover` skill, section 8). Failures of the provider itself skip it only in part: with a `provider` in `[roles.goal_review]`, a provider that cannot be used (missing executable, failed launch, login, usage limit) is held and the review moves to the other provider, no attention; without one, only Claude's login or usage limit is spared (the review waits in the `queue_hold` ask, whose `done` reviews it again), while a missing executable or a failed launch shows as `goal review by hand` and needs `goal review ID` once fixed. A goal review's `approve_goal` ask is an ask (`reference/asks.md`); while the supervisor applies its answer it shows as `applying the answer of ask <id> (runtime)`.
  - `request a plan for the draft` (`kind` `draft_planner_exhausted`): the runtime opened its planners for a draft the runtime or a job registered (a receipt's follow_up, a goal's gap) and none adopted, dropped or asked about it; no runtime planner opens for it again. Tell the person; on their word record a planning request that names it, `"$DAGQ" request add --text '<the person's words>' --ref task:N`, whose runtime planner adopts or drops it (or asks). Or the person decides it in their own terminal (no `DAGQ_ROLE`): `edit`, `lint` and `submit`, or `cancel` and `note`. A draft a planner kept with `keep_draft` shows no attention and waits as a draft the same way until the person asks for such a request.
  - `request a plan for the finding` (`kind` `finding_planner_exhausted`, the finding's task or run if any, `last_error` `finding N: <summary>`): the runtime opened its planners for a finding marked for a proposal (the observer's mark, or a person's `propose` answer) and none submitted, dismissed or asked about it. Tell the person; on their word record a planning request that names it, `"$DAGQ" request add --text '<the person's words>' --ref finding:N`, whose runtime planner submits a remedy (`submit ... --finding N`) or declines. Or the person decides it in their own terminal (no `DAGQ_ROLE`): `submit ... --finding N`, or `finding dismiss N --reason`.
  - `request a plan for the waiting tasks` (`kind` `dependency_stranded`, `status` `stranded`, on the stranded task, `last_error` `task A of goal G (closed abandoned) will not complete; tasks B, C wait on it`): a task of a closed goal was left `draft` / `submitted` / `ready` (approve withheld it, or the goal was abandoned) while `ready` / `submitted` tasks of open goals wait on it, directly or through other unfinished tasks. It never completes, so they are never claimed. The runtime does not hold them. Tell the person; on their word record a planning request that names the stranded task, `"$DAGQ" request add --text '<the person's words>' --ref task:A`, whose runtime planner decides whether to remove the dependency, cancel the waiting tasks or take the work up again in an open goal; or the person decides that in their own terminal (no `DAGQ_ROLE`). `watch` tells it once per set of waiting tasks; `status` shows it until nothing waits.
  - `install tool` (`kind` `run_env_program_missing`, `status` `missing`, no run or task, `last_error` the variable, its value and the PATH): a program the `[run.env]` of `dagq.toml` names cannot be executed on the supervisor's PATH. The supervisor claims no task and lands no run until it can (the runs in flight go on); the person installs the program where that PATH finds it, or has a planner register a task that takes the variable out of `dagq.toml`. It disappears when the supervisor finds it (`run_env_program_found`). `up` refuses to start a supervisor while it is missing, and `doctor`'s `run_env` shows what resolved where.
  - `check the e2e host` (`kind` `run_e2e_finished` with `attention: true`, an `awaiting_integration` run, `last_error` the e2e's error): the runtime could not run the e2e a reviewed run needs before landing `run_e2e::UNAVAILABLE_ATTENTION` (3) times in a row (each `run_e2e_finished` with `outcome: unavailable`: it or the rerun of its failed tests could not start or passed its time limit; ADR-t1233-2, `docs/design/supervisor-lifecycle/review.md`, "流せないとき"). The person checks the host: whether cmux answers `cmux ping`, whether the `[run.env]` of `dagq.toml` can be read (`dagq doctor`'s `run_env`), and whether the host's e2e lock file (`<data dir>/e2e.lock`) can be opened. The runtime keeps the run's slot and lease and keeps retrying (every 300 seconds); the attention clears by itself once an e2e runs (passed or failed), so the person does not close it and no ask opens. If the supervisor drains or hands off while the run waits to retry, it releases the lease without waiting, and the run stays `awaiting_integration` as `review and integrate` for the person (`dagq-recover`, section 6; check its `run_e2e_finished` before integrating by hand). A failed e2e is no attention: the run is `needs_session` (`run_e2e_failed`, code `e2e_failed`) and resumed.
  - `stop the dead landing's processes` (`kind` `landing_release_stuck`, an `integrating` run, `last_error` the pids left): a landing whose supervisor died left processes in the run's worktree that the runtime, after waiting their grace (30 minutes), could not stop by pid (or could not list for an hour). The run holds the integration slot until they end. Show the person the pids (`dagq show TASK --full` has the event's `processes`); the person stops those pids, never by name or pattern (after `survived_stop` the runtime no longer tries), and the next supervisor pass releases the run to `awaiting_integration`: a run whose review passed or that was approved queues to land again without a person, any other is reviewed again (`docs/design/supervisor-lifecycle/supervise.md`, step 12). It clears once the run leaves `integrating`.
  - `push main`: an `integrated` run (its task `completed`) whose push of `main` to `origin` failed (`kind` `push_failed`, `last_error` the Git error) with no successful push since.
  - `fix the push command` (`kind` `kpi_push_abandoned`, no run or task, `reason_category` `recovery_failed`): the KPI push command of `host.toml`'s `[push]` (a daily or weekly summary, or a target breach; `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/dagq/reference/kpi.md`) failed one message three times (`kpi_push_failed` has each exit code and the stderr tail) and the message was dropped. Claims, landings and the reports go on. The person fixes the command or its service; it clears only when a later push succeeds (`kpi_push_sent`), usually the next day's summary, and cannot be closed by hand.
  - `dagq service status` (`kind` `queue_service_down`, no run or task): the supervisor could not keep the queue service running (`status` `start_failed`: it did not start, `last_error` says why; `restart_limit`: it was started again 3 times in 10 minutes and still stopped). New claims and the queue's jobs wait meanwhile; runs in flight go on. Show the person `dagq service status` and `<queue dir>/service/service.log`; at their word, run `dagq service start` (or `up` again). It clears once the service runs (`queue_service_started` or `queue_service_running`).
  - `dagq broker status` (`kind` `broker_unhealthy`, no run or task): with `[broker]` mode `preferred`, the supervisor could not make the queue's resource broker ready (`status` is the failure's code: `machine_busy` when a Podman machine of the person's runs, `podman_missing`, `image_build_failed`, ...) or its health failed again after the automatic restart (`status` `unhealthy`; the restart itself is an `auto_repaired` with `repair: broker_restart`). Claims and landings go on without the broker. Show the person `dagq broker status`; the supervisor tries again by itself, and it clears once the broker runs (`broker_started` or `broker_healthy`).
  - `recover run`: a `claimed` / `starting` / `running` / `validating` / `integrating` run without a lease, given up by its supervisor (`kind` `runtime_error` with `lease_released: true`). Nothing adopts it; once its session's processes are gone the supervisor recovers it and hands it to the recovery job, and while they live, handle it with the `dagq-recover` skill. It disappears once recovered. If `watch` reported it but `status` shows the run at rest, `status` wins.
  - `exit the session` (`kind` `runtime_error`, the run `awaiting_integration` or unfinished without a lease): the supervisor gave the run up while it kept the worker's session open through validation, review, revise or its `/exit`, and could not send that session `/exit` (the event's `session.exit` is `failed`, with the `error`). A run given up whose `/exit` did get there (`session.exit` `sent` or `requested_before`) shows its own attention at once. The person sends `/exit` in the run's workspace (the `dagq-recover` skill, section 2); once the session exited, the run shows `review and integrate` or `recover run` again.
  - `report the update` (`update_installed`, a queue event with no task; it does not stay in `status`): the supervisor replaced the pinned binary by itself (`--auto-update`). Tell the person its `version`, `commit` (or `release`) and any `reason` / `message`, and that sessions reopened after it load the updated plugin (`watch.md`). Nothing to do; a failed update is an `update_failed` ask instead.
  - `report the review`, `check the failed review`: the throughput review's notices (`watch.md`, "The throughput review's notice").
  - A worker provider that cannot be used is no attention of its own: its runs move to the other provider by themselves (`provider_switched`). The person acts only on a `queue_hold` ask (log in, or wait for the limit; `asks.md`). Codex's hold opens no ask: `status`'s `supervisors[].provider_hold` shows it until its `retry_at`. A headless run's workspace has no screen and takes no keys (`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/dagq/reference/provider.md`).
  - `restart supervisor`: `kind` `supervisor_stale` (with its `pid`) or `supervisor_stopped` (nothing registered); `up` on the person's word.
  - `answer ask <id>`: an open ask (`kind` `ask_opened`, `status` `open`, with `ask_id`); the inbox's to answer. A run whose exit request timed out is no attention of its own: the runtime retries the `/exit`, answers a known dialog, or closes the workspace and lands a reviewed, clean run whose `/exit` never got through; what is left goes to the recovery job (alert `stuck_exit`). Only when the job cannot fix it does the supervisor open a `stuck_exit` ask (options `exit` / `wait`) for the inbox; its answer is carried out as the `dagq-recover` skill's `reference/stuck-exit.md` says, and the supervisor closes it once the session exits (that `ask_answered` carries `runtime_closed: true` and is no `watch` event).
  - A worker's session idle without a receipt is no attention of its own: the supervisor nudges it once, then opens a `stalled` ask (below). Once answered `wait` while the supervisor holds the run, it shows `applying the answer of ask <id> (runtime)`; `intervene` shows `read the answer of ask <id> and close it`.
  - `read the answer of ask <id> and close it`: an answered ask nobody closed and the runtime does not apply (`kind` `ask_answered`, `status` `answered`); the person's answer is carried out through the inbox. A `stuck_exit` answered `exit`, an `answer_prompt`, a `stalled` answered `intervene`, or the person's own text: carry it out as the `dagq-recover` skill's `reference/session.md` says, then `"$DAGQ" ask close <id>`. `wait`, or nothing to do: just `ask close <id>`. It disappears after `ask close <id>`.
  - `delivering the answer of ask <id> (runtime)`: an answered `worker_question` of a run whose session takes answers (`running`, a review's send-back, or a resume of a `needs_session` run); the supervisor types the answer into the worker's terminal once the worker is idle after asking (after the run is back in a slot when it waited outside one), closes the ask and records `ask_delivered`. Nothing to do; its `ask_answered` is not a `watch` event.
  - `send the answer of ask <id> to the worker and close it`: an answered `worker_question` the supervisor could not type (`kind` `ask_delivery_failed`, tried once), or whose run's session no longer takes answers. Handle it as the `dagq-recover` skill's `reference/session.md` says.
- `status --role <inbox|planner>` keeps only the attention for that role: all of it is the inbox's (ADR-0044 decision 17), none a planner's.
- `asks`: the open asks, each with `id`, `kind`, `question` (first 200 characters, `…` when cut), `task_id`, `run_id`, `asked_by` and `age_secs`.
- `cursor`: the newest event id.
- `language` (only with `--role inbox` or `--role planner`): the language to write for people in, `tag` and `source` (`repository`, `user` or `unset`), and the `instruction` to follow when one is set (the `dagq` skill's section 5).

## Runs waiting for a person (outside the slots)

A run whose live session only waits for a person's answer or hand leaves its `--parallel` slot while it waits (ADR-0071, which replaced ADR-0062), so the free slot claims another task; nights with unanswered asks no longer fill the slots. The run keeps its lease, its session and its status, and makes no attention of its own: its ask is the attention.

- Which asks make a wait: a `worker_question`, `answer_prompt` or `stalled` ask while the worker works (`phase` `session`); a `stuck_exit` or `answer_prompt` ask while its `/exit` waits (`exit`); a `worker_question` or `answer_prompt` ask during a review's send-back (`revise`) or a resume of a `needs_session` run (`resume`). A `stuck_exit` ask of a still `running` run (the older path) keeps its slot.
- Each `waiting` entry: `run_id`, `task_id`, `asks` (`[{id, kind}]`), `phase`, `status` (the run's own status), `state`, `since`, `waited_secs`, and for a returning run `ended_at` and `cause`. `state: waiting` waits for the person; `state: returning` has its wait over and waits for a free slot to go on.
- `waiting.count` per supervisor is waiting plus returning runs, the number `--max-waiting` bounds; `returning` is how many of them are returning; `limit` is the supervisor's `--max-waiting` (null for a registration from an older binary).
- `supervise --max-waiting N` / `up --max-waiting N` (default 4; `0` keeps every run in its slot): once `count` reaches the limit, a new ask does not start a wait: the supervisor records `run_waiting_deferred` once for that ask and the run stays in its slot, as before waits existed, until the count drops below the limit.
- What happens after an answer: an answered or closed `worker_question` ends the wait (`cause` `answered`), and so does the session exiting (`session_exited`: a `stuck_exit` answered `exit` and carried out, a session that died). Such a run is `returning` and goes back to a slot when one is free, oldest first, before any new claim; then the supervisor types the worker's answer, or sees the exit through and lands, reviews it or hands it to the recovery job as the `stuck_exit` question said. A dialog that went away (`dialog_cleared`), a session that moved on by itself (a new idle, prompt or receipt: `session_moved`), a login hold (`queue_hold`) or a silent wrapper (`wrapper_silent`) returns the run at once, even beyond `--parallel` (`run_slot_regained` with `over_parallel: true`). A send-back or resume stage restarts its timeout when the run is back; it does not time out while it waits.
- Events: `run_waiting_started`, `run_waiting_ask_added`, `run_waiting_ended` (`cause`, `waited_secs`), `run_slot_regained` (`slot_wait_secs`, `over_parallel`), `run_waiting_deferred`. A supervisor that takes the run over (a handoff or an adopt) goes on with the same wait from these events.
- A drain (`down`, `down --wait`, `install --allow-breaking`, `up` draining a supervisor from before the handoff) waits for waiting runs too, so it ends only once their asks are answered or their sessions exit. An exec handoff (`up` handing the supervisor over to another build, `handoff: true`) does not wait for them: the new process rebuilds the waits.

## A `stalled` ask

The supervisor's detection of a worker's session that stopped without a receipt (ADR-0043 decision 1; the steps are the `dagq-recover` skill's `reference/stalled.md`):

- When: the first session (`phase: session`) ended its turn and stays idle with no receipt, no unclosed `worker_question` or `answer_prompt`, no dialog and no login hold for `[stall].idle_without_receipt_secs` of `dagq.toml` (default 1200 seconds). Background work still running counts as idle. The supervisor first types one nudge (`stall_nudged`: write the receipt, ask a `worker_question`, or say what the background work is waited for); only when the session stays idle as long again after it does the ask open (a nudge that could not be typed goes to the ask on the next poll) (`asked_by` `supervisor`, `reason_category` `recovery_failed`, one open per run).
- The question: run, task, workspace, `reason: idle_without_receipt`, `phase: session`, the idle seconds and the seconds since the nudge, the background tasks left running (`- <description> (<id>): <command>`, or none), and the last lines of the screen. Show all of it to the person.
- A recovery job that escalates a live run's `long_background` or `idle_process` alert opens a `stalled` ask too (`alert: ...` in the question, the job's diagnosis and `reason_category`, and its own options after `wait` / `intervene`); it is answered and closed the same way.
- A text the supervisor typed that the session did not take opens a `stalled` ask too, with `reason: send_unconfirmed` (below, "A text or a request the session did not take").
- Options: `wait` leaves the session alone. The supervisor applies it (`applying the answer of ask <id> (runtime)`), closes the ask and counts the idle again from then; if the session stays idle another threshold a new `stalled` ask opens, without another nudge. With no supervisor holding the run it comes back as `read the answer of ask <id> and close it`: `ask close <id>`. `intervene` (or other text) means a person steps in: it comes back as `read the answer of ask <id> and close it`, carried out on the person's word with `dagq-recover` (read the screen, check or stop the background work through the session, type an instruction, or `/exit` the run so the recovery job takes it), then `ask close <id>`; no new `stalled` ask opens until the session ends a turn after that. `propose` records a finding for the stall and a runtime planner takes it; the session is left alone as for `wait`. It shows `read the answer of ask <id> and close it` until the supervisor closes it; with no supervisor holding the run, `ask close <id>` (read as `wait`).
- The supervisor closes the ask itself (`the session moved on; closed by the runtime` / `the session exited; closed by the runtime`) once the session ends a new turn, writes its receipt, opens a `worker_question`, or exits, and when its recovery job takes the ended run (`the run was triaged; closed by the runtime`); after `intervene`, once the session ends a turn after the answer. `answer` then reports the ask is not open. `recover` does not close it.
- Each detection's outcome is a `stall_resolved` event (`resolved_by_nudge`, `escalated`, `answered_wait`, `answered_intervene`, `resolved_by_itself`, `run_ended`), counted by `stats`' `stall_thresholds`.

## A text or a request the session did not take

Everything the supervisor types into a live session (a resolution request, a worker's answer, a send-back, a nudge, `/exit`) is checked on the screen afterwards (the runtime's side: `docs/design/supervisor-lifecycle/session-send.md`). Three events say a typed text was not taken; none is an attention by itself:

- `submit_unconfirmed`: the text still sits in the input box after the supervisor pressed Enter again three times (`what`, `retries`, `excerpt`). A `/exit` left this way waits out the exit timeout instead, as the `stuck_exit` alert (recovery job first, then a `stuck_exit` ask).
- `submit_not_started`: `[stall].send_confirm_secs` (default 60 s) after the send there is no idle marker, no input marker, no receipt and no sign of work on the screen; a dialog came up (`dialog`), the text stays in the box, or it was lost and typed once more without effect (`resent`).
- `input_not_ready`: a resumed session's input box did not get ready for the resolution request within the agent's registration timeout (`waited_secs`; `prompt` names a dialog holding it). The request is not typed yet.

Who asks the person:

- `submit_unconfirmed` of a text and `submit_not_started` (first session, send-back or resume) go to the session's recovery job first (alert `stalled`, `reason: send_unconfirmed`). Only when it cannot fix them does a `stalled` ask open: the question names the workspace, says what happened to which text (for example `the resolution request the supervisor typed stays in the input box after 4 Enters, not submitted`), gives the job's diagnosis and ends with the screen; options `wait` / `intervene` / `propose` plus the job's. `wait` leaves the session alone and, unlike an idle, is not counted again: there is one job and one ask per send. `intervene` means a person presses Enter or types the text again in that workspace (`dagq-recover`'s `reference/session.md`).
- `input_not_ready` opens an `answer_prompt` ask at once (`asked_by` `supervisor`, `reason_category` `recovery_failed`, no options): the question says the resolution request is not sent, asks what to send (for example `enter` to press Enter, or the text to type) and ends with the screen. Write the person's words as the answer; it comes back as `read the answer of ask <id> and close it` and is carried out in that workspace with `dagq-recover`'s `reference/session.md`. The supervisor closes the ask itself once the box gets ready, just before it types the request (`the input box got ready and the request was sent; closed by the runtime`) or the session exits.

## watch and events

`"$DAGQ" watch --after <cursor> [--timeout 600 | --until-attention] [--interval 2]` blocks until an attention event arrives after the cursor or the supervisors' registrations or `alive` / `stale` change, then returns `{events, supervisors_changed, supervisors, cursor}`. On timeout `events` is empty and the cursor is unchanged; with `--until-attention` there is no timeout, and the inbox runs it that way (`watch.md`). `--role inbox` wakes for every attention event and for the supervisors, except that `update_installed` and an hourly `throughput_review_reported` alone do not wake it: they come back, oldest first, among the `events` of the next return (ADR-t1418-1); `--role planner` wakes for nothing, and a watch with no role wakes for every attention event. Asks ride the same cursor: registering one writes `ask_opened`, answering it `ask_answered`. `"$DAGQ" events --after <cursor>` returns the same attention events without waiting (`--all` for every kind, `--limit N`, default 100). Each event is compact: `status`, `exit_code`, `ask_id`, `reason` cut to 300 characters, and `next`.

## asks

`"$DAGQ" asks` lists the asks nobody closed, oldest first, with every field (`question`, `options`, `answer`, `answered_at`, `closed_at`). `--open` keeps the unanswered ones, `--role inbox` both the open ones and the answered ones nobody closed, `--all` adds closed asks.

Registering one (`"$DAGQ" ask --kind <kind> --because <reason> --question <text> [--option <text>]... (--run RUN_ID | --task ID)`):

- `--because` is why a person is needed (ADR-0047 decision 41), and is required: `scope` (the acceptance, the scope, an ADR or a goal's decision), `discard` (whether to throw work away) or `recovery_failed` (what should have fixed it could not). A question that fits none of them is no ask: leave it as a note. `authentication` and `cost` asks are the runtime's `queue_hold` asks: one per queue, with the runs and headless jobs they hold in `affected` (a run or a job that stops at the same login or usage limit joins it and records `ask_updated`; a job's failure is no attention of its own while it is listed). While an `authentication` ask or a usage-limit `cost` ask (`subject: usage_limit`) is open, the supervisor claims no new run and starts no headless job (review, recovery, plan review, goal review, observer; `claim_held` with reason `authentication` / `usage_limit`); the runs in flight keep their leases. Its answer is the runtime's (`applying the answer of ask <id> (runtime)`): `done` (the person logged in, or the limit is back) ends the hold, types a fixed text to go on into each held session and starts again the recovery, plan review and goal review jobs that failed at it; `cancel_affected` gives the held runs up like an abandon (lease released, `runtime_error` with code `hold_canceled`), so each comes back as `recover run` for the person to decide with `dagq-recover`. Show the question with its `Affected:` line before a person answers `cancel_affected`. The disk's `cost` ask (`subject: disk`, options `done` / `wait`) comes once the free disk space stays short of what claims and landings need after the supervisor cleaned the ended runs' build outputs; its `affected` are the runs whose landing waits. The supervisor applies the answer (`done`: it cleans and checks again; `wait`: it does not ask again) and closes the ask itself once there is room. Every ask, `status` entry, attention and ask event carries its `reason_category`; show it to the person with the question.

- `--run` for a question about one run, `--task` for one about a task as a whole; one of them is required. `approve_landing` is a doubt about a landing (the supervisor's, or a review by hand), `answer_prompt` a dialog a session stopped at (the supervisor's), `decide` anything else; `worker_question` is the workers' own kind (a worker registers it with `--run` and `--topic <code>`, repeated, primary first: what was left undecided, from the codes the worker's prompt and `dagq ask --help` list (ADR-t947-2); required for it and refused on any other kind; then it stops), `stuck_exit` the supervisor's and `blocked` the observer's.
- Write the question so the person can answer it without your session's context: the task, what you found, the options and what each leads to.
- The same run (or task) and kind is registered once: asking again returns the open ask with `created: false`.
- A new ask sends one `cmux notify` to the inbox workspace (`notified: true`); asking again notifies nobody. A failed notification does not fail the ask: it stays registered with `notified: false` and `notify_error`, and the inbox still sees it through `watch --role inbox`.
- `ask close <id>` marks an answered ask read. An unanswered ask cannot be closed: to withdraw one, answer it yourself first (`"$DAGQ" answer <id> --text "withdrawn: <why>"`), then close it.

## stats

`"$DAGQ" stats [--since CURSOR] [--goal ID] [--full]` reads run events only; the field list is in the `dagq` skill's `reference/inspect.md`. For load problems look at:

- `backend_failures`: `{count, by_op, retried, exhausted, retried_by_op, exhausted_by_op, max_load_avg, max_slots, by_load_band}` — cmux calls that failed or timed out (30 s each) in the window: after `--since` up to `next_cursor`, otherwise since the oldest returned run started. `by_op` counts per call (`create`, `create_named`, `capture`, `close`, `send_exit`, `exists`, `ensure_group`); `retried` counts the failed attempts that were retried (non-null `retry_after_ms`) and `exhausted` the failures that gave up (null or missing `retry_after_ms`, which includes events from before the retry), with `retried + exhausted = count` and each split per op in `retried_by_op` / `exhausted_by_op`; `max_load_avg` is the highest 1-minute load average recorded with one (null when unavailable) and `max_slots` the most runs the supervisor held then. Each failure is a `backend_call_failed` event (`op`, `workspace_id`, `timeout_secs`, `error`, `load_avg`, `slots`, `parallel`, `attempt`, `max_attempts`, `retry_after_ms`: a `capture`, `exists` or a `send_text` that left no trace on the screen is retried with backoff after a timeout, and every failed attempt is one event), on its run when it was for one and on no task for `up` / `down` / the workspace group. `cleanup_failed`, `screen_capture_failed` and `exit_request_timed_out` are still recorded as before; the first two also produce a `backend_call_failed`, while `exit_request_timed_out` (a session that did not exit) does not — only a failed `send_exit` does.
- `waiting`: `{started: {<ask_kind>: count}, waited: {<ask_kind>: {count, total_secs, median_secs, max_secs}}, slot_wait: {count, total_secs, median_secs, max_secs}, over_parallel, deferred}` — the waits started in the window per ask kind, how long they waited (`total_secs` is the slot time a waiting run would have held), how long each run waited to get its slot back (about 0 for one that returned at once), how many came back beyond `--parallel`, and how often `--max-waiting` deferred a wait. The alert `idle_slots` counts no waiting or returning run as busy.
- The alert `backend_failures` (`value` the count, `threshold` 2, no task or run): two or more failures in the window, counted from `count` (retried attempts included). A high `max_load_avg` with it is the evidence for proposing a lower `--parallel`; the observer reads it from `stats` and does not fix it.

## show

`"$DAGQ" show ID` prints the task, only the latest run, the latest 10 events (`--events N` for more) with the gist of their payload, and long texts cut to 300 characters ending in `…` with `truncated: true`. `show ID --full` adds `run_dir`, earlier runs and whole event payloads such as a receipt. `"$DAGQ" list --status in_progress` finds the tasks worth a `show`: each entry's `latest_run` gives the newest run's `id` and `status`.

## Run states

- `claimed` / `starting` / `running`: in progress. Events: `lease_acquired`, `workspace_created`, `agent_started`, `receipt_observed`, `session_idle_observed`, `exit_requested`.
- `validating`: the session went idle after its receipt (it stays open) or exited; the supervisor checks the receipt, the commit, a clean worktree and required evidence. It does not run the verification commands; `integrate` runs them once after its rebase.
- `awaiting_integration`: accepted. While the supervisor holds its lease it reviews the run with the session still open (`review_started` / `review_finished` with `reason_codes` and `primary_code`, `revise_requested` / `revise_finished`); a pass lands it, a concern closes the session and opens an `approve_landing` ask, an unreadable verdict is reviewed once more (`review_retried`), and a failed review closes it and opens an `approve_landing` ask with the failure (`review_failed` with `ask_id`). `result_commit` on `branch` is what was reviewed; the worktree and branch are kept until the landing.
- `integrating`: an `integrate` process holds the queue's single integration slot. If its process died (`doctor` shows `lease_stale: true`), the `dagq-recover` skill returns the run to `awaiting_integration`.
- `needs_session`: `integrate` could not land it (rebase conflict, or a verification command failed after the rebase); `last_error` says why. The supervisor resumes its session (up to three times, not counting resumes for a rebase conflict alone after a passed review; `resume_started` / `resume_finished` events; after that it retries on the old branch, or makes the run `failed` for the recovery job), then lands it if `integrate` was called for it (`integration_approved`), or else validates and reviews it with the resumed session open, like the worker's. A `send_back` answer to a review's `approve_landing` ask also makes a run `needs_session` (`landing_decided`).
- `integrated`: landed on `main`; the task is `completed`.
- `failed`: rejected or exited nonzero; `last_error` says why. The supervisor's recovery job retries or resumes it, or a `decide` ask goes to the inbox; its workspace is closed and the worktree kept.
- unfinished with `last_error` and no lease (a `runtime_error` event with `lease_released: true`): the supervisor gave the run up; it recovers the run once the session is gone (the `dagq-recover` skill without a supervisor). After a provisioning failure the supervisor stops claiming, drains and exits nonzero; launchd restarts it, so fix the cause (cmux, Git) and check `status`.
- `interrupted`: recovered; the recovery job handles it like a `failed` run.

Completion is decided only by these states. A Stop hook, an idle session or a `receipt.json` in `run_dir` is not success: `validation_finished` records the supervisor's verdict. One run's failure never changes another run.
