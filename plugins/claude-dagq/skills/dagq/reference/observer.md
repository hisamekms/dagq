# Findings: what the observer saw, and how they are decided

The observer is a headless job the supervisor starts every `--observe-interval` seconds (default 3600; 0 disables it) and once a day for the last 24 hours (`--observe-daily`). It reads `stats` since its cursor, the `open` / `proposed` findings, the latest notes, the open asks and the graph, and records what goes wrong as **findings** (ADR-0044 decision 18). It writes no note, goal or task, and it never changes a run, task or goal; the queue refuses that from `DAGQ_ROLE=observer`. When nothing but its own events happened since its last successful observation, it starts no agent and records a `skipped` observation. Run it by hand with `"$DAGQ" observe [--since CURSOR] [--daily]`, or print its prompt with `--dry-run`.

## What a finding is

One record per problem: `kind` (a slug: `stall`, `failure`, `wait`, `capacity`, `threshold`, `conflict_hotspot`, ...), a target (`run`, `task`, `goal` or `queue`) and a `subject` within it, `summary` and `detail`, `impact` (`high` / `normal` / `low`), `first_seen` / `last_seen`, `occurrences`, `evidence` (event IDs), `status` (`open` / `proposed` / `resolved` / `dismissed`, with `status_reason`), the linked proposal, and the mark asking for one (`propose_reason`). The same kind, target and subject recorded again updates the finding instead of adding one: new evidence adds an occurrence and moves `last_seen`, a `resolved` finding becomes `open` again (the remedy did not work), and a record that changes nothing writes nothing.

## Read

- `"$DAGQ" findings` lists the `open` and `proposed` findings, biggest impact first (impact, occurrences, last seen), each with `proposal_status` and `open_asks`. `--all` or `--status S,...`, `--kind K,...`, `--task ID` / `--run RUN_ID` / `--goal ID` / `--queue` narrow it; `findings ID` shows one in any status; `--full` adds the evidence events whole (`evidence_events`).
- `"$DAGQ" events --all --full --run RUN_ID` (or `--task`, `--goal`, `--kind KIND`, `--since` / `--until`) reads the events around a finding; `"$DAGQ" timeline RUN_ID` shows where a run's time went, with a reason for each gap (`reference/inspect.md`).
- `"$DAGQ" observe --history [--limit N]` lists the observations, newest first: `outcome`, `skipped`, `input: {since, through}` (the events it read), the findings it recorded or updated, its asks, `duration_secs` and its `dir` under `<queue dir>/observer/` (`prompt.md`, `input.json`, `output.out`, `output.err` (older jobs: `output.log`)). An outcome other than `succeeded` left the cursor, so the next observation reads the same window again. `prompt_bytes`, `prompt_limit` and `prompt_sections` (each section's `bytes`, `kept` and `omitted`) tell what its prompt came to.
- `"$DAGQ" observe --input OBSERVATION [--section PATH] [--offset N] [--limit N]` reads an observation's whole input (OBSERVATION is the name of its `dir`), including what its prompt left out to stay within its limits: without `--section` the sections with their bytes, else one section (`stats.runs`, `findings`, ...), a list a page at a time.

## Threshold findings: reading `stall_thresholds` and `running_alerts`

The supervisor detects stalled sessions by the `[stall]` settings (ADR-0043); the observer reads how those detections went and records a finding when a threshold looks wrong (ADR-0043 decision 6, ADR-0044 decision 21). Read the same fields when deciding a `threshold` finding (`"$DAGQ" stats`, `"$DAGQ" findings --kind threshold`).

- `stall_thresholds` has one entry per setting: `idle_without_receipt_secs` (receipt-less idle: nudge, then `stalled` ask), `send_confirm_secs` (a sent text not taken: Enter retry, resend, ask), `background_alert_secs` (`long_background`) and `idle_process_secs` (`idle_process`: the recovery job's ask, or `left_to_phase` when it left the process to the phase's own timeout). Each entry has `threshold_secs` (the value now), `detections`, `by_detection` (per `nudge` / `recovery` / `ask` / `enter_retry` / `resend` / `left_to_phase`, with outcomes), `outcomes`, `detected_after_secs` / `resolved_after_secs` (count, median, max), `preempted` (a person stepped in by input or `recover` before any detection, per `via`), `by_threshold_secs` (outcomes per value the detections were made with) and `running_alerts`.
- Many `answered_wait` (the ask's answer was to wait): the threshold is probably too early. Many `preempted`: probably too late. `resolved_by_nudge` / `resolved_by_enter` / `resolved_by_resend`: the detection works. `pending`: no outcome yet; do not judge on it. Compare the outcomes before and after a change of the value with `by_threshold_secs`.
- In `running_alerts`, an `idle_without_receipt` alert with `nudged: false` and `asked: false` past its `threshold` suggests the supervisor missed the stall.
- The observer records a revisit as `finding record --kind threshold --subject <setting name>` and adds `--propose` when it recurs; it never changes the threshold. Changing a value is the user's decision (`[stall]` in `dagq.toml`; defaults in `docs/design/supervisor-lifecycle/stall-thresholds.md`).

## Session time: reading `sessions`

The `stats` the observer reads carry the Claude session time per kind (`sessions.by_kind`, and `sessions` per run and goal; fields in `reference/inspect.md`, "Claude session time"). A kind whose `open` grows while its `active_ratio` falls spends its time waiting, not working; many `active_unavailable` spans mean the transcript could not be read, or inbox and planner spans just closed wait for the supervisor's intake (the active time is missing, not zero); many `inferred` spans mean sessions ended without a seen exit (recovered, triaged, or the workspace was gone). Read them when deciding a `capacity` or `wait` finding.

## KPI findings: kind `kpi` and the improvement limit

The observer also reads the KPIs (`reference/kpi.md`) of the last 7 days and 4 weeks, with the targets of `dagq.toml` and `host.toml` (ADR-0051 decisions 24 to 26). Its input `kpi` has `targets` (per target and period: `state`, `streak`, `breach_since`, the judged `values`), `breaches`, `trend` (per period, its runs, its marks and the KPIs that worsened) and `forecast` (below); `improvements` is the limit below. It copies numbers from them and computes none.

- A target in `breach` (off target `breach_periods` days or `breach_weeks` weeks in a row) becomes a finding `--kind kpi --queue --subject '<KPI>/<stratum>'` (e.g. `phase.work/area=<area>`) with the open `kpi_breach_started` event as evidence; the day and the week of the same KPI and stratum are one finding. `missed` and `not_judged` make none, and a breach is never a `blocked` ask. When the target is `ok` again, the observer resolves the finding. It marks one `--propose` when the impact and the length call for a remedy.
- **Forecast findings: kind `forecast`** (ADR-0070 decision 5). `kpi.forecast` in the input lists, per day and week with a scored snapshot, how the completion forecasts came out against the finishes: the counts (`details`) and the `forecast.*` KPIs per stratum (`p50_error` and `p50_error_ratio`, positive when late; `p50_abs_error`; `p90_hit_rate`; `late_rate` / `early_rate`; `marks=0` is the method's own error, `marks=1+` includes plan changes). A bias that goes on (the p50 always short or long, a low p90 hit rate) is judged only as a breach of a `forecast.*` target in `[kpi.targets]`; such a breach becomes `--kind forecast --queue --subject '<metric>/<stratum>'` without the `forecast.` prefix (e.g. `p90_hit_rate/all`), never a `kpi` finding, and follows the same evidence, resolve, `--propose` and improvement-limit rules. Read one with `"$DAGQ" kpi` and `"$DAGQ" forecast`.
- Read a `kpi` finding with `"$DAGQ" kpi` for its KPI and stratum and `"$DAGQ" marks` around `breach_since`: a change that took effect there may be the cause, or the remedy already in place.
- **Improvement limit.** While the unfinished proposals the runtime's planners submitted for findings, plus the runtime planners open for open findings, reach `[kpi] max_improvement_proposals` of `dagq.toml` (default 2), the supervisor opens no planner for another marked finding; it stays `open` and waits, oldest mark first. `"$DAGQ" findings` shows it as `improvements` (`running`, `limit`, `reached`, `waiting` with `reason: improvement_limit`). A proposal a person submits with `--finding` from their own planner is not counted.
- **Priority.** An improvement's tasks are `normal` or `low`: when plan review passes a proposal linked to a finding, it lowers its `high` or higher tasks to `normal`.

## Where a finding goes

1. **A proposal, without a person.** The observer marks a finding that needs a remedy (`finding record --propose '<why>'`: it recurs or costs much). The supervisor then opens a runtime planner for it, which checks `search` / `related` for work already queued or done and either submits tasks on an open goal or a new draft goal (`submit ... --finding ID`: the finding becomes `proposed`), dismisses it, or asks the person with a `planner_question`. Plan review checks the proposal like any other and asks the person only on a `concern`. When the proposal ends the runtime marks the finding `resolved` (a task completed) or `open` again without its mark (everything was canceled). After three runtime planners left it undecided, the inbox gets `request a plan for the finding`: on the person's word it records a planning request that names it (`request add --ref finding:N`), whose runtime planner takes it as above.
2. **An ask, only when its reading needs a person** (ADR-t451-1 decision 2). A finding whose reading is to wait or leave it stays a finding, with that reading in its detail: no ask; read those with `"$DAGQ" findings`. The observer raises `ask --kind blocked --finding ID --because <scope|discard|recovery_failed> --recommend <option> --confidence <high|low>` only when the reading needs a person's decision (scope, discard) or a person's hands the runtime and the recovery job cannot lend (recovery_failed), one open ask per finding; the queue refuses a blocked ask without `--recommend`. Each observation's `observe_finished` counts the findings it left without an ask (`findings_without_ask`), and `stats`' `recommendations.observer` sets them next to the blocked asks. The runtime adds the options `propose` (make it a proposal: the runtime marks the finding and a runtime planner takes it as in 1) and `dismiss`, and applies those answers itself; the inbox shows the ask. A `stalled` ask carries `propose` too, which records a finding for the stalled run first.
3. **Decided on a person's word.** People no longer open planners (ADR-t1394-1). A person who wants a finding acted on asks the inbox, which records a planning request that names it (`request add --text '<their words>' --ref finding:N`); its runtime planner submits a proposal for it (`submit ... --finding ID`), dismisses it (`"$DAGQ" finding dismiss ID --reason '...'`), or leaves it to the observer's mark. `finding resolve ID --reason '...'` closes one that no longer happens. A runtime planner opened for anything may also dismiss or resolve a finding its work settles. A `request a plan for the finding` attention is instead taken by a planning request the inbox records (`request add --ref finding:N`, as in 1), or decided by the person in their own terminal (no `DAGQ_ROLE`).

A note (`note` / `notes`) stays the person's and planners' free-text memo; nothing waits on it. Observers of older binaries wrote notes and draft goals; those remain as records: such a draft goal is decided like any draft on the person's word: through a request that names it (`request add --ref goal:N`), whose runtime planner submits it (`submit --goal ID`) or drops it (`goal close ID --verdict abandoned`), or by the person in their own terminal (no `DAGQ_ROLE`).
